steps:
  - Char the onion and ginger until lightly roasted — cut side down on a flame or under a hot grill, until blackened in patches. Rinse off any loose ash.
  - Dry roast the cloves, star anise, fennel seeds, coriander seeds, peppercorns and cinnamon in a pan until fragrant. Tie them into a spice bag, or leave them loose and strain the broth later.
  - Add the chicken, charred onion and ginger, spices, water, salt, fish sauce and a little brown sugar to a pot. Simmer for about an hour — don't let it boil — skimming off any foam that rises.
  - Remove the chicken and shred it. Strain the broth and adjust the seasoning: it will usually want more fish sauce and a little more salt.

Two steps decide whether this tastes like phở or like chicken soup with noodles in it. Neither of them is complicated, and both are easy to skip.

The first is charring the onion and ginger. Put them cut side down on a flame or under a hot grill until they blacken in patches. It caramelises the sugars in the onion and burns off the raw edge of the ginger, and that sweetness sits underneath everything else in the bowl. Skip it and the broth tastes flat no matter how long you simmer it.

The second is temperature. The broth should simmer, never boil. A hard boil churns the fat into the liquid and turns it cloudy, and phở is judged on clarity as much as flavour. You want the surface barely moving — a bubble every second or two. Skim the foam off in the first ten minutes and keep skimming until it stops rising.

The rest is an hour of waiting, and a table full of herbs and chilli so everyone builds their own bowl.
